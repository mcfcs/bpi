# Voice-over script: ALAMKansya demo video

Group Oreo Bread · timed to `ALAMKansya-Demo-Edited.mp4` (5:27) · also fits the plain `ALAMKansya-Demo.mp4`

**How to use it:** play the video and start reading each line when the timer reaches its timestamp. Each line is sized to fit its slot at a relaxed pace (about 140 words a minute), with a little room to spare; if you finish early, just pause until the next cue. The bracketed notes say what is on screen.

---

## 0:00 – 0:09 · Hook and Act 1 card

[Title card: "What does BPI's research already say?", then "ACT 1"]

> What does BPI's research already say? Finding out usually means searching files and waiting on an analyst. Here's ALAMKansya.

## 0:09 – 0:14 · Ben asks

[Ben's ask page; the cursor moves to the question box]

> This is Ben, in SME Banking. He just asks.

## 0:14 – 0:38 · The brief is prepared

[He clicks the example question; the progress steps tick through; zoom on the steps]

> He picks the question about why SME owners drop off loan applications. ALAMKansya searches only the approved studies his team may read, by meaning, not keywords. It compares who each study asked, writes the answer from those findings only, and then checks every sentence against its source.

## 0:38 – 0:56 · The answer first

[The brief appears; zoom on the answer; Plain language, then Executive]

> The answer comes first. It can be written for an executive, an analyst, or in plain language, and the facts stay exactly the same in every version.

## 0:56 – 1:08 · Figures and citations

[Zoom on the key figures and the citation chip; callout: "Numbers copied from the studies"]

> Every figure is copied straight from a study. The AI never writes a number. And every statement shows the study and page it came from.

## 1:08 – 1:30 · Confidence, who was asked, gaps

[Confidence line, then the "Who was asked" and "Gaps" sections]

> The brief also says how sure it is, based on how strong the studies are. It shows exactly who was surveyed, where and when, and it names what the evidence doesn't cover.

## 1:30 – 1:58 · A follow-up

[Ben types "What about owners in Mindanao?" and sends it]

> Ben asks a follow-up: what about owners in Mindanao? He doesn't have to repeat himself. The follow-up keeps the original subject and searches the same studies his team can read.

## 1:58 – 2:07 · An honest answer

[Zoom on the answer; callout: "Honest: no Mindanao evidence yet"]

> And it's honest. No approved study covers Mindanao owners yet, so it says so.

## 2:07 – 2:29 · A question nothing answers

[Ben asks "How do sari-sari store owners use supplier credit?"; callout: "No answer? It says so"]

> Now a question no study answers: how sari-sari store owners use supplier credit. Instead of guessing, ALAMKansya says no approved finding supports an answer, and it remembers that Ben asked.

## 2:29 – 2:37 · Act 2 card

[Card: "ACT 2 · Ana, in Strategy & Insights, reviews and approves"]

> Now, the other side of the desk.

## 2:37 – 2:53 · Ana's desk

[Ana's ask page and her desk cards]

> This is Ana, an analyst in Strategy and Insights. Her desk shows what's waiting: new reports to review, questions from teams, and access requests.

## 2:53 – 3:17 · Checks before approval

[Cebu report; zoom on the phone number, then the overlapping studies; callouts]

> Before any study goes live, two checks run. This report quotes someone with their phone number, so approval waits until Ana edits it or confirms it identifies no one. And it overlaps three approved studies, so she can see if it's new research or a repeat.

## 3:17 – 3:25 · Ana approves

[Mindanao report; she types a note and clicks Approve]

> She adds a note and approves the Mindanao report. The app never approves on its own.

## 3:25 – 3:38 · Someone is told

[Zoom on the message; callout: "1 earlier asker told"]

> The app tells her this study answers one earlier question nobody could answer before, and that the person who asked has been told.

## 3:38 – 3:58 · The gap map

[Gap map; zoom on "Where new research would be used first", then the Asked and Unanswered columns]

> This is the gap map: what teams ask about, what research covers for which places, and which questions no study answered yet. It shows where new research would be used first.

## 3:58 – 4:17 · The audit log

[Audit log tiles, then the entries with Ana's approval and note]

> And every action is recorded: who did it, when, and their note. Nobody can edit or delete an entry. The app even counts its own impact, like questions answered without an analyst.

## 4:17 – 4:26 · Act 3 card

[Card: "ACT 3 · Ben is told"]

> Back to Ben.

## 4:26 – 4:39 · Ben is told

[Ben's ask page with the badge; spotlight on the green alert; callout: "Ben is told automatically"]

> Ben asked before the research existed. Now it exists, and he's told, automatically, without anyone remembering to follow up.

## 4:39 – 5:09 · The new answer

[He clicks See the answer; the brief is prepared again; zoom on the answer citing the new study]

> One click asks his question again. This time there's an answer, drawn from the new Mindanao study and cited to its page. It's marked as focus-group evidence, so it says what people described, not how common it is.

## 5:09 – 5:27 · Close

[Closing caption, then the end card: "ALAMKansya · Ang alkansya ng kaalaman · Ask · Review · Approve · Told"]

> That's the loop: ask, review, approve, told. ALAMKansya: ang alkansya ng kaalaman. Thank you.
