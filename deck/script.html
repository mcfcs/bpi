Pitch script: Evidence Brief

BPI DataWave 2026 · Strategy & Insights challenge · recorded presentation, 10–15 minutes

Target: about 13½ minutes. Slide numbers match Evidence-Brief-Pitch-Deck.pdf (18 slides). The time on each heading is the running total at the end of that slide, at about 140 words a minute. If a rehearsal runs long, cut slide 14 first (25 s), then demo step 2.

Before you record

  • Start the app (./scripts/tailnet.ps1 -Dev), sign in to AWS, and check http://localhost:10012/api/health shows "llm_ready":true and "search_ready":true.

  • Reset the demo data so the Mindanao report is waiting in the review queue: ./scripts/tailnet.ps1 -Stop, then backend/.venv/Scripts/python backend/scripts/dev_db.py --reset, then start again.

  • Browser at 110% zoom, light theme, other tabs closed. Have two windows ready: one signed in as Ben (SME Banking), one as Ana (S&I), using the persona menu at the top right.

  • Suggested split: Speaker 1 slides 1–7, Speaker 2 slides 8–11, Speaker 3 slides 12–18.

Slide 1 · Evidence Brief0:25

Good day. We're Oreo Bread, and this is Evidence Brief. Anyone in a business unit can ask a question in plain words and, in about eleven seconds, get an answer from BPI's approved research, with every claim cited to the page it came from.

Slide 2 · The problem1:20

In our consultations, S&I described one problem three ways.

Studies live in silos: a study belongs to the unit that paid for it, so another unit that could use it often doesn't know it exists. Search needs the exact words: SharePoint matches keywords, so a question asked differently misses the right study. And so business units ask S&I directly, and analysts spend their time on questions existing studies already answer. Sometimes two units even commission research that reaches the same answer.

Business units compete with fintechs on speed. A decision made without research BPI already owns is slower, more expensive, or both.

Slide 3 · The slow loop2:10

Say SME Banking wants to know why owners abandon the online loan application. "Loan" returns dozens of files. "Drop-off" misses the study that says "abandon". The best study belongs to another unit, so they ask for access file by file. Then they ask S&I and wait, or decide anyway, or commission research that repeats what BPI already knows.

S&I told us the time this takes can't be quantified. That is the real problem: nobody can see how many questions existing research could already answer.

Slide 4 · The solution2:50

Evidence Brief replaces that loop with one step. Ask in English, Filipino or Taglish. It searches by meaning across every approved study your team may read, answers first, shows the figures, who was asked and what's missing, and cites every claim. You can ask a follow-up, or send it to an analyst in one click.

Slide 5 · The brief on one screen3:25

The answer comes in three wordings: executive, analyst or plain language, all from the same evidence. The key figures come straight from the studies; the AI writes none of them. Confidence is worked out from study strength. And everything else, the charts, quotes and gaps, is one click away.

Slide 6 · Demo5:55

Let me show you.

[Switch to the browser. About 2½ minutes. Say what you do as you do it.]

  1. As Ben, SME Banking. Click the example "Why do SME owners drop off during online loan applications?"

    You can see each step as it works: searching, comparing, writing, checking. Documentation first, approval time second, with ranges from three studies and high confidence.

  2. In Ask a follow-up, type "What about owners in Mindanao?"

    The follow-up keeps the subject. And it's honest: no approved study covers Mindanao owners yet.

  3. Ask a question, then type "How do sari-sari store owners use supplier credit?"

    No approved study answers this. Ben's question is remembered.

  4. Switch to Ana, S&I, open Review queue, then Cebu SME Digital Onboarding Focus Groups 2026.

    Before a study goes live, two checks run. A quote contains a phone number, so approval waits until the analyst edits it or confirms it identifies no one. And it overlaps three approved studies, so S&I can see whether this is new research or a repeat.

  5. Back to Review queue, open Mindanao Sari-sari Store Credit Focus Groups 2026, click Approve.

    It answers one earlier question nobody could answer before, and that person has been told.

  6. Switch to Ben. Point to the badge and the green notice; click See the answer.

    Ben asked before the research existed. Now it's here, and he's told, without anyone remembering to ask again.

[Back to the slides.]

Slide 7 · The loop6:35

That's the loop we close. A new study is checked: personal data flagged, overlap shown, the analyst decides. Whoever asked a question it now answers is told, searched as them, under their own access. And S&I sees it all on a gap map: what teams ask, what's covered, and where new research would be used first. None of this adds an AI call: it's pattern rules, stored search data and code.

Slide 8 · Role of AI7:25

So where is the AI? In two places. Finding the right findings by meaning, and Claude writing the plain-language answer from those findings, citing each one.

Everything else is code and the database. Permissions are enforced before the AI sees anything. Code compares the studies, checks every claim the AI writes, and builds the charts and confidence from stored values. The AI never supplies a number, never decides that studies disagree, never sees a restricted study, and never approves anything.

Slide 9 · Why AI here8:05

We used AI only where it measurably beats a simpler tool. Word matching finds three in four reworded questions; meaning search finds ninety-four percent. Templates pad answers with unrelated figures; Claude keeps to what the studies measured. On real BSP reports, rules found almost nothing; Claude found up to eighty-three percent of findings, each with its page. For numbers, access, confidence and personal data, we kept plain code, because those have to be exact.

Slide 10 · Risks and safeguards9:00

For each risk, a check. Invented figures: numbers come from the database and every citation is checked. Focus-group opinions dressed as statistics: qualitative claims must be hedged. A restricted study leaking: the database filters before the AI sees anything, tested on every change. Personal data in a report: flagged, and approval waits. Hidden instructions in an upload: an analyst reviews every report first. Over-trust: confidence and gaps come first, and every action goes into an audit log nobody can edit. And prompts stay inside AWS.

Slide 11 · People decide9:35

BPI asked for guardrails from day one, and people making the final call. So there are four checkpoints: an analyst approves every study, after the privacy and overlap checks; the owning team decides every access request; any brief goes to an analyst in one click; and nothing is commissioned until a person sends it.

Slide 12 · Benefits10:10

Business units get answers in seconds, find studies from other units, and are told when new research answers an old question. S&I gets fewer repeat questions, duplicates caught at review, and a gap map of where to research next. And the bank gets more from research it already paid for.

Slide 13 · What we measured10:50

On the prototype: ninety-four percent of reworded questions find the right study, ninety-three percent as the top result. A full brief takes a median of eleven seconds, and in eighteen runs on Amazon Bedrock none fell back to templates. An adversarial test leaked nothing restricted. This is synthetic data: it shows the mechanism works. Value has to be measured with real users.

Slide 14 · Proving the impact11:15

So we propose an eight-week pilot with S&I and one unit: half of questions answered without an analyst, ninety-five percent accuracy rated blind by S&I, zero leaks, and time to answer against today.

Slide 15 · Feasible today11:55

It's feasible today, not someday. It's already built and working on real Bedrock models, with two hundred and fifteen automated tests. No data migration: it uses the reports S&I already has. No model training. Standard parts bank IT already runs. One AI call per brief, about two US cents.

Slide 16 · Built to scale12:35

And it scales by design. Cost follows questions, not studies, because each report is processed once. Search stays fast as the library grows. A new business unit is a data change, not a code change. The model is one setting to swap, with templates if it's down. From five hundred to fifty thousand briefs a month, the design stays the same: only the database and an AI bill of twelve to eleven hundred dollars.

Slide 17 · From one unit to the bank13:00

Two weeks to set up a BPI sandbox. Eight weeks of pilot. Then a department, then the bank.

Slide 18 · Close13:25

So our ask: let business units ask the research. From S&I, real anonymised questions and sign-off on our rules. From IT and Risk, a sandbox and a decision on data residency. Thank you.

Timing check: about 13½ minutes at a relaxed pace, about 12½ briskly. The demo is the part most likely to run over; if the AI is slow, open the brief from "Your recent briefs" instead of asking live.
